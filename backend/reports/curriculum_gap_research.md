# Curriculum gap research: 10 missing subjects

Research date: 2026-09-08. Curriculum baseline: 449 topics in `dev.db`.

**57 proposed topics / 95 resource entries across 10 areas. Every URL below was fetched with
`app.content.audit_resource_links.fetch`, and where a `section` is claimed the headings were
read on the live page.** ~90 distinct URLs were checked; 10 candidates were rejected (listed in
"Rejected candidates" at the bottom) and 10 more were rewritten to their post-redirect
`final_url` so they will not trip `REDIRECTED` in the audit.

No `raw.githubusercontent.com` URL appears in this report.

## Honest gaps — where I would NOT stake my reputation

Read this first. Four of the ten areas have a soft spot.

| Area | The gap | What I did instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **6. Retrieval quality — "choose a vector store"** | There is **no vendor-neutral, authoritative page** that compares FAISS vs Chroma vs pgvector vs a managed service. Every page that claims to is marketing by one of the four. I will not link one. | I split the decision into two things that *do* have honest sources: **choosing an ANN index** (FAISS's own guidelines + ANN-Benchmarks, which is objective measured data) and **when Postgres is enough** (the pgvector README). The learner ends up able to make the choice without ever reading a comparison table. If the owner wants a literal "FAISS vs Chroma vs pgvector" lesson, that lesson has to be *written*, not linked. |
| **3. Cloud — cost control and GPU pricing** | GPU rental prices move monthly, and there is no authoritative vendor-neutral page on "what an AI workload actually costs". AWS/GCP pricing pages are authoritative but vendor-locked and long. | I anchored cost on **mechanisms that do not go stale** (spot vs on-demand, right-sizing) rather than numbers. The AWS Well-Architected cost pillar is the least-marketing option but is a whitepaper, so I scoped it to Abstract + Introduction only. Live price comparison remains an un-linkable gap. |
| **4. AI/ML security — PII handling** | The obvious tool anchor, **Microsoft Presidio, has been orphaned**: `microsoft.github.io/presidio` now returns a bare "This page has moved" page and the repo appears to have left the Microsoft org. I will not link it. NIST SP 800-122 is authoritative but is from 2010 and pre-LLM. | PRIMARY is OWASP LLM02 (LLM-specific, current, exactly on topic); REFERENCE is Google Cloud's de-identification concepts page, which is vendor-hosted but is the clearest *technique* taxonomy (masking / tokenisation / format-preserving encryption / date shifting) I could verify. This is the weakest link in an otherwise strong area. |
| **5. Inference performance — CPU vs GPU inference** | No single authoritative page explains "CPU vs GPU inference" as such. The honest framing is *memory-bandwidth-bound vs compute-bound decoding*, and the best page on that is a personal blog (kipply's), not a vendor doc. | I used kipply's *Transformer Inference Arithmetic* as PRIMARY for the mechanism (most-cited treatment, and it shows the arithmetic) with the ONNX Runtime quantization doc as the concrete CPU-side reference. A learner will understand *why* CPU decode is slow, but there is no official page saying so. |

Two smaller notes:

- **7. Data quality gets 5 topics, not 6.** Outside the scikit-learn user guide there is no
  authoritative single page on data quality as a discipline — it is book-length or vendor
  material. Five sharp topics on leakage, inconsistent preprocessing, imputation, outliers and
  imbalance is the honest ceiling. I did not pad it.
- **Sutton & Barto (Area 2 reference) is served over `http://` only** — `https://incompleteideas.net`
  presents a self-signed certificate (`CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_FAILED`). Store the `http://` URL
  verbatim; "fixing" it to https will break it.
